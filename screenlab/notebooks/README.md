# Notebooks

`explore_runs.ipynb` runs a few backtests and one background job into a run store, then lists, pivots, compares, plots, queries (SQL) and reruns them. Written for a non-programmer; about 35 seconds end to end.

* Install: `pip install numpy pandas matplotlib jupyterlab nbclient ipykernel skfolio` (skfolio supplies the bundled S&P 500 data; run from the repository root so `screenlab` imports, or `pip install -e` it).
* Interactive: `jupyter lab screenlab/notebooks/explore_runs.ipynb`, then Run All.
* Headless, with outputs saved in place: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -m nbclient --inplace explore_runs.ipynb` (or `jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace explore_runs.ipynb`).
* It writes a local SQLite file, `screenlab/notebooks/runs.sqlite` (plus `-wal`/`-shm` side files while open), deleted and recreated on every run from the top. It is git-ignored; change `STORE_PATH` / `START_FRESH` in the first code cell. Nothing leaves your machine.
* Column and unit reference for that file: `screenlab/schema/runstore.linkml.yaml` (guarded against drift by `tests/test_schema.py`).

## `montecarlo.ipynb`

How wide is the range of outcomes of momentum and low volatility (each with and without the fixed trend exit) against the S&P 500, on 1,000 resampled futures? Plain-language walk through the data card and warnings, the quantile table, the fan chart, 10-year growth histograms against the index, the paired per-path differences and the worst-fall (drawdown) histograms, then "how to read this" and "what this cannot tell you" (survivorship, +10 points a year; price-only index). About 20 seconds; it writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('montecarlo.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'montecarlo.ipynb')"`. Its charts come from `research/montecarlo_histograms.py`, so the notebook and the research script share one set of plotting functions.

## `accounts_and_foreign.ipynb`

Where should a portfolio live, a taxable account, a TFSA or an RRSP? Runs `lab.account_compare(...)` (the same portfolio and the passive index under each account, Canadian model for Alberta, other income $90,000 a year, RRSP withdrawal taxed at 30%) on a Globe and Mail dividend screen over the synthetic Canadian market and on two screens over the bundled S&P 500 sample (US names, with an assumed 2% yield so that foreign withholding exists). It shows the comparison table (after-tax CAGR, tax drag, after-tax excess over the equally taxed index, withholding lost / credited / exempt, tax paid on dividends, gains and the RRSP withdrawal, rank), a bar chart of after-tax CAGR by account, the withholding treatment matrix for each kind of holding, and a reading guide with what it ignores (contribution room, the RRSP deduction refund, AMT). About 20 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('accounts_and_foreign.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'accounts_and_foreign.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_account_compare.py` executes its code cells too. The Accounts tab of the TUI ([../tui/README.md](../tui/README.md)) shows the same comparison for the selected scenario.

## `tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb`

Foreign holdings and currency, with hand-built markets and no random simulation. (1) The withholding matrix as a heatmap (lost / credited back / exempt) and a bar chart of after-tax income for a Canadian eligible-dividend stock, a US stock and a Canadian-listed ETF on US stocks with the same 4% yield, in a taxable account, a TFSA and an RRSP; the heatmap is read from the tax stage's own ledger and checked against the documented matrix. (2) The same US exposure through a US-listed or a Canadian-listed ETF: after-tax CAGR by account and the yearly withholding stack. (3) Currency and the cost base: the US price up 10% while USDCAD falls 15% is a 6.5% loss in Canadian dollars (and the reverse case, -5% and +15%, is a 9.25% gain), with the USD price, CAD price and cost base charted, the realized-gain rows in both currencies and the warning shown when no FX series is loaded. (4) A pointer to the bundled S&P 500 sample (assumed 2% yield) in the three accounts, with after-tax curves, and what `securities.csv` changes. (5) A reading guide with what it ignores (non-USD FX, fund NAV already net of withholding, T1135, estate and PFIC issues). About 15 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py::test_foreign_holdings_notebook_runs` executes its code cells too.

## `tax_where_to_hold.ipynb`

Where does the tax come from, and what do the account and the bracket change? One Globe and Mail dividend screen (`stable_dividend`) on the synthetic Canadian market, Canadian model for Alberta, other income $90,000, always next to the passive index taxed the same way. Seven charts: after-tax growth curves (before and after tax, per account), the tax paid each year stacked by kind, grouped drag bars (growth before vs after tax); the RRSP convention (after-tax growth against the withdrawal rate 20-50%, and tax drag against the holding length of 3 to 11 years, with why short windows punish the RRSP and why the deduction refund is not modelled); the bracket (marginal rates on ordinary income, eligible and non-eligible dividends and capital gains for Alberta and British Columbia, and after-tax growth at other income of $30,000 / $90,000 / $200,000 in each province); then a reading guide with what it ignores (contribution room, the RRSP refund, AMT, 2025 tables for every year, British Columbia constants marked UNVERIFIED). About 20-30 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_where_to_hold.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_where_to_hold.ipynb')"`. Its charts come from `research/tax_plots.py` (shared with the other tax notebooks); `tests/test_tax_plots.py` smoke-tests that module and `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py` executes the notebook's code cells.

## `tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb`

Tax-loss harvesting and the superficial-loss rule, with no Monte Carlo: one history, the tax mechanics only. (1) One name, one loss: a hand-built market where a name falls 20%, sold at year end, the 30-day window shaded, and three variants side by side (second pick, stay out, allow denial: what is held when, loss realized vs denied, cost base after). (2) A Globe screen (`dogs`) on the synthetic market in a taxable account, harvest off vs on: after-tax curves, tax paid each year, `Harvested/yr` and `TaxDeferred/yr`, the loss carry-forward balance, a timeline of the sales, and nine runs of threshold (5/10/20%) by `when` (year end / rebalance / both). (3) The 3-year carry-back: refund per year as bars, and the case where the loss outruns three years. (4) An exit policy parking in cash: the exit sale recorded as a harvest, re-entry on day S+10 (second pick) or S+31 (the name back). (5) A reading guide with what it ignores (spouse and affiliated accounts, identical-property ETFs, settlement dates, the "still held at window end" test, TFSA/RRSP where `TaxDeferred/yr` is meaningless). About 20 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb')"`. Charts come from `research/tax_plots.py`; `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py` runs its code cells.

# Notebooks

`explore_runs.ipynb` runs a few backtests and one background job into a run store, then lists, pivots, compares, plots, queries (SQL) and reruns them. Written for a non-programmer; about 35 seconds end to end.

* Install: `pip install numpy pandas matplotlib jupyterlab nbclient ipykernel skfolio` (skfolio supplies the bundled S&P 500 data; run from the repository root so `screenlab` imports, or `pip install -e` it).
* Interactive: `jupyter lab screenlab/notebooks/explore_runs.ipynb`, then Run All.
* Headless, with outputs saved in place: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -m nbclient --inplace explore_runs.ipynb` (or `jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace explore_runs.ipynb`).
* It writes a local SQLite file, `screenlab/notebooks/runs.sqlite` (plus `-wal`/`-shm` side files while open), deleted and recreated on every run from the top. It is git-ignored; change `STORE_PATH` / `START_FRESH` in the first code cell. Nothing leaves your machine.
* Column and unit reference for that file: `screenlab/schema/runstore.linkml.yaml` (guarded against drift by `tests/test_schema.py`).

## `montecarlo.ipynb`

How wide is the range of outcomes of momentum and low volatility (each with and without the fixed trend exit) against the S&P 500, on 1,000 resampled futures? Plain-language walk through the data card and warnings, the quantile table, the fan chart, 10-year growth histograms against the index, the paired per-path differences and the worst-fall (drawdown) histograms, then "how to read this" and "what this cannot tell you" (survivorship, +10 points a year; price-only index). About 20 seconds; it writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('montecarlo.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'montecarlo.ipynb')"`. Its charts come from `research/montecarlo_histograms.py`, so the notebook and the research script share one set of plotting functions.

## `accounts_and_foreign.ipynb`

Where should a portfolio live, a taxable account, a TFSA or an RRSP? Runs `lab.account_compare(...)` (the same portfolio and the passive index under each account, Canadian model for Alberta, other income $90,000 a year, RRSP withdrawal taxed at 30%) on a Globe and Mail dividend screen over the synthetic Canadian market and on two screens over the bundled S&P 500 sample (US names, with an assumed 2% yield so that foreign withholding exists). It shows the comparison table (after-tax CAGR, tax drag, after-tax excess over the equally taxed index, withholding lost / credited / exempt, tax paid on dividends, gains and the RRSP withdrawal, rank), a bar chart of after-tax CAGR by account, the withholding treatment matrix for each kind of holding, and a reading guide with what it ignores (contribution room, the RRSP deduction refund, AMT). About 20 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('accounts_and_foreign.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'accounts_and_foreign.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_account_compare.py` executes its code cells too. The Accounts tab of the TUI ([../tui/README.md](../tui/README.md)) shows the same comparison for the selected scenario.

## `tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb`

Foreign holdings and currency on **real data**: the point-in-time S&P 500 panel 1990-2022 (`Lab.real_sp500(start="1990-01-01", end="2022-12-30")`, about 1,250 names with real per-stock dividends, delisted names included) and the real daily USDCAD. **Needs the panel, which is an unlicensed mixed-source dataset (CRSP-derived, then Yahoo / Tiingo / Quandl WIKI) downloaded with `make data-panel` into the git-ignored `screenlab/data/panel/` for local use only; it is not redistributed, so keep the executed notebook (its outputs embed figures derived from the panel) local too.** The test skips when `data/panel/prices.parquet` is absent. The first run builds the panel cache (about 15 s); with the cache the notebook takes about 45 seconds, offline, and writes no files.

A two-paragraph **Summary** under the title, computed from the notebook's own variables, quantifies the withholding cost by account and wrapper (dollars per $100,000 started in 1990, and points of growth a year) and what the currency did to the taxable gain over 33 years, each against the base case. Every section opens with its question and its link to the previous section, then tables and charts, then a closing paragraph headed "What the evidence says" written from the run's numbers. Every chart has a legend naming every line, bar, marker and shading. **The base case on every chart and table is buy and hold of the S&P 500 total-return index (`SP500_TR`) in the same account** (grey, dashed; the rows marked "Buy and hold the index"); in Section 4, where the question is the currency itself, it is the same trades taxed in US dollars, with the index sold on the last day beside it. Tax: Alberta, other income $90,000, `rrsp_withdrawal_rate=0.30`, `TaxConfig(macro=lab.macro)` for the Canadian-dollar basis, the 2025 tables applied to every year.

(1) The data: the data card, a table of what the panel holds, USDCAD 1990-2022 against the buy-day rate (shaded where the US dollar is worth less), and the index and the dogs in both currencies. (2) Withholding on real dividends: the `dogs` screen (highest yields, so withholding matters most) and the index under taxable / TFSA / RRSP, dollars lost / credited / exempt per year on $100,000 as stacked bars with the index as the grey dashed line, after-tax growth by account against the index, and the withholding matrix read from the tax stage's ledger and checked against `docs/TAXES.md`. (3) The wrapper: the same dogs exposure as a US-listed ETF (the tax stage treats `etf_us` exactly like the US stock, checked in the notebook) and as a Canadian-listed ETF (a model: `security_meta` overridden on a copy of the dataset with `wrapper="etf_ca"`, `underlying_domicile="US"`, the net asset value kept in US dollars so only the withholding differs), per account. (4) Currency and the cost base: the dogs' realized gain per year on the Canadian-dollar basis and with the currency ignored (bar pairs, the years the sign flipped), the tax difference including a final sale, USDCAD by year and by stretch of years, and the index's Canadian-dollar cost base rebuilt from the library's dividend stream (the library taxes the passive index in US dollars). (5) The same for `momentum_12m`, as tables. (6) A reading guide: what is real (prices, dividends, FX, membership), what is approximate (2025 tax tables, the delisting haircut, trailing-yield distress names, yields paid pro rata between rebalances, the ETF wrapper as a model of the same holdings), what it ignores, and the licence.

Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3',timeout=600).execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py::test_foreign_holdings_notebook_runs` executes its code cells (90 s limit) and checks the Summary, a "What the evidence says" in every section, the forbidden phrases, and a legend and the base case on every figure. Its heatmap and legend options come from `research/tax_plots.py` (`withholding_matrix(legend=, frame=)`, marked `# W2:`).

## `tax_where_to_hold.ipynb`

Where does the tax come from, and what do the account and the bracket change? Real data, no simulation: the dogs of the S&P 500 (the ten highest trailing dividend yields of the whole index, rebalanced yearly) on the point-in-time S&P 500 panel, 1990-2022 (500 names on any date, 1,248 over the window, 631 delisted), with `momentum_12m` and `stable_high_yield` as comparison rows (value and buyback screens cannot run: the panel has no market cap). Canadian model for Alberta, other income $90,000, USD gains measured in Canadian dollars with the real USDCAD series, 15% US withholding on every dividend. The base case in every chart and table is buy and hold of the S&P 500 total return (`SP500_TR`) in the same account, taxed the same way (grey dashed, "Buy and hold the index (after tax)").

**The data is local use only.** The panel is an unlicensed, mixed-source dataset (CRSP-derived 1968-2006, then Yahoo / Tiingo / Quandl WIKI). It is not in the repository and is not redistributed: download it yourself with `make data-panel` (or `screenlab-data panel`; about 235 MB from github.com and release-assets.githubusercontent.com) into the git-ignored `screenlab/data/panel/`, and do not commit or share it or outputs that embed it. The notebook raises a clear error when the files are absent, and `test_where_to_hold_notebook_runs` is skipped. It loads `Lab.real_sp500(start="1990-01-01", end="2022-12-30")` with exactly those arguments so the on-disk cache in `data/panel/cache/` is hit (the first load takes about 15 seconds and 2 GB of memory, a cached load a few seconds).

A two-paragraph **Summary** at the top is computed from the notebook's own variables: what each account cost the dogs in points of growth and dollars on $100,000 over 1990-2022 against the index in the same account, the RRSP convention (withdrawal rate, break-even rates), the holding period and the bracket. Six sections, each an opening paragraph (the question and its link to the previous section), figures and tables, and a closing paragraph headed "What the evidence says": (1) the data and why it is bias-free (data card printed, before-tax growth and risk of the three rules and the index); (2) the three accounts for the dogs (after-tax curves, yearly tax stacks of dividends / gains / withholding / RRSP withdrawal, drag bars, and a table of where the tax came from); (3) the RRSP withdrawal rate 20-50% and the holding period (start years 1990 / 2000 / 2010 / 2015 to 2022) on real history; (4) the bracket (other income $30k / $90k / $200k, Alberta and British Columbia) and the marginal-rate curves, with the index's US dividends taxed as ordinary income as the base; (5) the same question for the other two screens (account ranking and gap to the index); (6) a reading guide: what is real, and what is approximate (the 2025 tax tables applied to every year, the -30% delisting haircut with a no-haircut bound, distressed high-yield names in the dogs, no market cap, the licence). Every chart has a legend naming every line, bar and mark.

About 60-70 seconds with the cache warm, offline once the panel is on disk, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_where_to_hold.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_where_to_hold.ipynb')"`. Its charts come from `research/tax_plots.py` (shared with the other tax notebooks; the `baseline=` and `legend=` arguments carry the common base case and the legends); `tests/test_tax_plots.py` smoke-tests that module and `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py` executes the notebook's code cells and checks its composition (summary, six closing paragraphs, forbidden phrases, a legend and the base case on every chart).

## `tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb`

Tax-loss harvesting and the superficial-loss rule, tested on **33 years of real data**: the point-in-time S&P 500 panel, 1990-2022 (`Lab.real_sp500(start="1990-01-01", end="2022-12-30")`: 1,248 names, 631 of them delisted, real per-stock dividends, the real total return `SP500_TR` as the benchmark). **Local use only.** The panel is an unlicensed mixed-source dataset (CRSP-derived, then Yahoo / Tiingo / Quandl WIKI); download it with `make data-panel` into the git-ignored `screenlab/data/panel/`, never commit or redistribute the files or results that embed them (the first cell of the notebook says so). Without `data/panel/prices.parquet` the notebook cannot run and `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py::test_loss_harvesting_notebook_runs` skips. Tax: `CanadianTax("AB", other_income=90000)`, taxable account; the model has 2025 tables only and they are applied to every year 1990-2022; prices are US dollars with no currency series. One history, no Monte Carlo.

It opens with a two-paragraph **Summary** computed from the notebook's own variables (so it cannot drift from the charts): what harvesting cost on each of three screens (turnover and trading cost, name-days out of the chosen names, denied losses in count and dollars) and what it was worth (tax saved in dollars, points of after-tax growth split into tax and before-tax return, carry-back refunds, and the result against buying and holding the index). Every section then follows one composition: the question it answers and how it follows from the previous section, the evidence, and a closing paragraph headed "What the evidence says" (including when the answer is "no effect" or "mostly luck"). Every chart has a legend that names every line, band, marker and shading, and every chart and table carries the base case: on the real data it is buying and holding `SP500_TR` in the same account (grey dashed, "Buy and hold the index (after tax)") next to the same screen with harvesting off, and on the hand-built one-name markets it is buying and holding the same name and never selling.

(1) One name, one loss: a hand-built illustration of second pick, stay out and allow denial against buy and hold of the name. (2) `dogs` (the ten highest trailing yields of the whole S&P 500, rebalanced yearly) 1990-2022, harvesting off vs on (10% at the year end, second pick, `on_repick="avoid"`) vs buy and hold of `SP500_TR`: table, after-tax curves, tax paid each year with carry-back refunds as negative bars, the carry-forward balance with the crash years shaded, the harvest timeline, and the cost (turnover, trading cost, name-days out, harvest sales not bought back, denied losses in count and dollars, loss sold and denied per year). (3) Nine runs of threshold (5/10/20%) by `when` (year end / rebalance / both), daily bars, as after-tax value against both base cases and as the zoom of the gain over harvesting off. (4) The same test on `momentum_12m` and `stable_high_yield`: pre-tax CAGR, after-tax CAGR off and on, the change split into tax and before-tax return, tax saved, sales, denied sales, turnover, with the index row. (5) Carry-back: two hand-built cases (refunds of exactly $13,000 and $18,000, $30,000 carried forward), then the crash years 2000-02 and 2008-09 on `dogs`: losses realized with harvesting off and on, refunds by year, and the balance still waiting five years later. (6) An exit policy parking in cash on a hand-built name: the exit sale recorded as a harvest, re-entry on day S+10 (second pick) or S+31, against buy and hold of the name. (7) A reading guide: what is real, what is approximate (delisting haircut of 30%, 2025 tables for every year, no currency, screens without a size cut, one history) and what the model leaves out (spouse and affiliated accounts, identical-property ETFs, settlement dates, TFSA/RRSP where `TaxDeferred/yr` is meaningless).

About 90 seconds with the cache present (the first call without cache adds about 20 seconds to build it); the nine-run grid on daily bars is the slowest part. Writes no files. Headless, with outputs saved in place: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3',timeout=600).execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb')"`. Charts come from `research/tax_plots.py` (`legend=`, `baseline=` and the refund bars of `tax_paid_stack`); `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py` runs its code cells and checks its composition.

## `plan_dca.ipynb`

What would a real investing plan have become? A dated plan on 33 years of real S&P 500 history (the point-in-time panel, `Lab.real_sp500(start="1990-01-01", end="2022-12-30")`, **local use only**: run `make data-panel` once): $10,000 on 1990-01-02, $500 a month indexed to CPI, two extraordinary investments ($25,000 in March 1999, $15,000 in March 2009) and withdrawals of $3,000 a month from January 2018, in the dogs of the S&P 500 against buying and holding the index with the same dollars on the same days, in a TFSA, an RRSP (30% withdrawal rate) and a taxable account (Alberta, other income $90,000). It opens with a two-paragraph **Summary** computed from the notebook's own variables, then seven sections, each a question, the analysis and "What the evidence says": the flows; the straight calculation (wealth against the money put in, pre- and after-tax, by account); the metrics (XIRR against TWR explained in one paragraph, alpha, beta, Sharpe, and the return counted before tax, after tax, after inflation and after both); invest on arrival against holding the cash until the next rebalance; the $1.5 million goal; the Monte Carlo (500 resampled paths: fan, terminal histograms, the probability of beating the index, the probability of missing the goal, sequence of returns); and a reading guide (US CPI and the US 10-year yield as stand-ins, the 2025 tax tables applied to every year, the delisting haircut, the licence). Every figure has a legend and the grey dashed index base case. About two and a half minutes with the cache present (the first call adds about a minute). Writes no files. Headless, with outputs saved in place: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('plan_dca.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3',timeout=900).execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'plan_dca.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py::test_plan_notebook_runs` runs its code cells (skipped when the panel is absent). The operations behind it are described in [../docs/PLAN.md](../docs/PLAN.md).

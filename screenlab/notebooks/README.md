# Notebooks

`explore_runs.ipynb` runs a few backtests and one background job into a run store, then lists, pivots, compares, plots, queries (SQL) and reruns them. Written for a non-programmer; about 35 seconds end to end.

* Install: `pip install numpy pandas matplotlib jupyterlab nbclient ipykernel skfolio` (skfolio supplies the bundled S&P 500 data; run from the repository root so `screenlab` imports, or `pip install -e` it).
* Interactive: `jupyter lab screenlab/notebooks/explore_runs.ipynb`, then Run All.
* Headless, with outputs saved in place: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -m nbclient --inplace explore_runs.ipynb` (or `jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace explore_runs.ipynb`).
* It writes a local SQLite file, `screenlab/notebooks/runs.sqlite` (plus `-wal`/`-shm` side files while open), deleted and recreated on every run from the top. It is git-ignored; change `STORE_PATH` / `START_FRESH` in the first code cell. Nothing leaves your machine.
* Column and unit reference for that file: `screenlab/schema/runstore.linkml.yaml` (guarded against drift by `tests/test_schema.py`).

## `montecarlo.ipynb`

How wide is the range of outcomes of momentum and low volatility (each with and without the fixed trend exit) against the S&P 500, on 1,000 resampled futures? Plain-language walk through the data card and warnings, the quantile table, the fan chart, 10-year growth histograms against the index, the paired per-path differences and the worst-fall (drawdown) histograms, then "how to read this" and "what this cannot tell you" (survivorship, +10 points a year; price-only index). About 20 seconds; it writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('montecarlo.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'montecarlo.ipynb')"`. Its charts come from `research/montecarlo_histograms.py`, so the notebook and the research script share one set of plotting functions.

## `accounts_and_foreign.ipynb`

Where should a portfolio live, a taxable account, a TFSA or an RRSP? Runs `lab.account_compare(...)` (the same portfolio and the passive index under each account, Canadian model for Alberta, other income $90,000 a year, RRSP withdrawal taxed at 30%) on a Globe and Mail dividend screen over the synthetic Canadian market and on two screens over the bundled S&P 500 sample (US names, with an assumed 2% yield so that foreign withholding exists). It shows the comparison table (after-tax CAGR, tax drag, after-tax excess over the equally taxed index, withholding lost / credited / exempt, tax paid on dividends, gains and the RRSP withdrawal, rank), a bar chart of after-tax CAGR by account, the withholding treatment matrix for each kind of holding, and a reading guide with what it ignores (contribution room, the RRSP deduction refund, AMT). About 20 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('accounts_and_foreign.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'accounts_and_foreign.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_account_compare.py` executes its code cells too. The Accounts tab of the TUI ([../tui/README.md](../tui/README.md)) shows the same comparison for the selected scenario.

## `tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb`

Foreign holdings and currency, with hand-built markets and no random simulation. A summary at the top quantifies what withholding costs by account and wrapper (dollars per $100,000 and points of growth a year) and what the currency does to the taxable gain, each against that section's base case. Every section opens with its question, then the tables and charts, then a closing paragraph headed "What the evidence says" written from the run's own numbers. Every chart has a legend, and every chart and table carries one base case drawn grey and dashed: in Sections 1 and 2 the same holding bought and held in a taxable account, in Section 3 the same stock with the exchange rate held at its buy-day value (the US-dollar price), in Section 4 buy and hold of the S&P 500 index in the same account. (1) The withholding matrix as a heatmap with a colour key (lost / credited back / exempt, the taxable column framed as the base) and a bar chart of after-tax income for a Canadian eligible-dividend stock, a US stock and a Canadian-listed ETF on US stocks with the same 4% yield, in a taxable account, a TFSA and an RRSP; the heatmap is read from the tax stage's own ledger and checked against the documented matrix. (2) The same US exposure through a US-listed or a Canadian-listed ETF: after-tax CAGR by account against the same ETF in a taxable account, and the yearly withholding stack. (3) Currency and the cost base: the US price up 10% while USDCAD falls 15% is a 6.5% loss in Canadian dollars (and the reverse case, -5% and +15%, is a 9.25% gain), with the US-dollar price (base), Canadian-dollar price and cost base charted, the realized gains with and without the currency, and the warning shown when no FX series is loaded. (4) The bundled S&P 500 sample (assumed 2% yield) in the three accounts, with after-tax curves against buy and hold of the index. (5) The findings together, with your own data (`securities.csv`) and what the notebook ignores (non-USD FX, fund NAV already net of withholding, T1135, estate and PFIC issues). About 15 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_foreign_holdings.ipynb')"`. `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py::test_foreign_holdings_notebook_runs` executes its code cells too.

## `tax_where_to_hold.ipynb`

Where does the tax come from, and what do the account and the bracket change? One Globe and Mail dividend screen (`stable_dividend`) on the synthetic Canadian market, Canadian model for Alberta, other income $90,000. The base case in every chart and table is buy and hold of the passive index in the same account, taxed the same way (grey dashed, "Buy and hold the index (after tax)"). A summary at the top quantifies, from the run's own variables, the cost of each account in points of after-tax CAGR and dollars on $100,000, the RRSP convention's effect and the bracket's effect, against the base case. Four sections, each with an opening paragraph, figures and tables, and a closing "What the evidence says": (1) what each account costs (after-tax growth curves, tax paid each year with the index's tax as a line, grouped drag bars with the index's after-tax growth as the base mark); (2) how much of the RRSP result is convention (after-tax growth against the withdrawal rate 20-50%, and tax drag against the holding length of 3 to 11 years, with the withdrawal rate at which the RRSP would match the taxable account, and why short windows punish the RRSP and the deduction refund is not modelled); (3) what the bracket and the province change (marginal rates by kind of income for Alberta and British Columbia with the index's income, eligible dividends, as the base, and after-tax growth at other income of $30,000 / $90,000 / $200,000); (4) the three results together (how far each assumption moved the answer), then what the model ignores (contribution room, the RRSP refund, AMT, 2025 tables for every year, British Columbia constants marked UNVERIFIED). About 20-30 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_where_to_hold.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_where_to_hold.ipynb')"`. Its charts come from `research/tax_plots.py` (shared with the other tax notebooks; the `baseline=` and `legend=` arguments carry the common base case and the legends); `tests/test_tax_plots.py` smoke-tests that module and `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py` executes the notebook's code cells.

## `tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb`

Tax-loss harvesting and the superficial-loss rule, with no Monte Carlo: one history, the tax mechanics only. It opens with a two-paragraph **Summary** computed from the notebook's own variables (so it cannot drift from the charts): what harvesting cost (turnover and trading cost, days out of the chosen names, denied losses, the extra trades) and what it was worth (tax saved in dollars and in points of after-tax growth, how much of the gain was tax and how much second-pick luck, the carry-back refund, and the result against buying and holding the passive index). Every section then follows one composition: the question it answers and why it follows from the previous section, the evidence, and a closing paragraph headed "What the evidence says" (including when the answer is "no effect" or "mostly luck"). Every chart has a legend that names every line, band, marker and shading, and every chart and table carries the base case: on the Globe screen it is buying and holding the passive index in the same account (the library's index convention, grey dashed, "Buy and hold the index (after tax)"), and on the one-name markets it is buying and holding the same name and never selling.

(1) One name, one loss: a hand-built market where a name falls 20% and is sold at year end, with account value, the loss banked for tax and what is held when under three variants (second pick, stay out, allow denial), against buy and hold of the name; the table shows loss realized vs denied and the cost base afterwards. (2) A Globe screen (`dogs`) on the synthetic market in a taxable Alberta account, harvest off vs on vs buy and hold of the index: a table (growth, tax drag, turnover, trading cost, tax paid, value of $100,000 after tax), after-tax curves, tax paid each year with refunds as negative bars, the loss carry-forward balance, a timeline of the sales, and nine runs of threshold (5/10/20%) by `when` (year end / rebalance / both) shown as after-tax value against both base cases and as the gain over harvesting off. (3) The 3-year carry-back: tax paid and refunded per year in two hand-built cases, and the cumulative tax of the section 2 run with its refund years. (4) An exit policy parking in cash: the exit sale recorded as a harvest, re-entry on day S+10 (second pick) or S+31 (the name back), against buy and hold of the name, with the cost of the window in forgone gains. (5) A reading guide with what the model ignores (spouse and affiliated accounts, identical-property ETFs, settlement dates, the "still held at window end" test, TFSA/RRSP where `TaxDeferred/yr` is meaningless) and a closing conclusion. The long setup and the runs sit in one collapsed code cell under the title, so the summary is the first thing read; the plotting cells stay visible. About 20 seconds, offline, writes no files. Headless: `cd screenlab/notebooks && python -c "import nbformat; from nbclient import NotebookClient as C; nb=nbformat.read('tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb',4); C(nb,kernel_name='python3').execute(); nbformat.write(nb,'tax_loss_harvesting.ipynb')"`. Charts come from `research/tax_plots.py` (`legend=`, `baseline=` and the refund bars of `tax_paid_stack` are the additions for this notebook); `tests/test_tax_notebooks.py` runs its code cells and checks its composition.
